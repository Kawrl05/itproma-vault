# SMART Objectives — Guide

**Project objectives** are the specific, smaller steps that lead to the broader **project goals**. Objectives should be **SMART**:

| Letter | Meaning | What to Ask |
|---|---|---|
| **S**pecific | Precisely what will be accomplished | What exactly, for whom, why? |
| **M**easurable | A number/metric to track | How will progress/success be measured? |
| **A**chievable | Realistic given resources/constraints | Can the team actually do this? |
| **R**elevant | Ties to the business case / project goal | Does this support the "why" of the project? |
| **T**ime-bound | Has a deadline or timeframe | By when? |

## How to Draft a Fully Expanded SMART Objective

A weak objective states an intent ("increase customer satisfaction"). A **fully expanded SMART objective** adds all five elements explicitly, e.g.:

> "**Increase** customer satisfaction ratings for R&M appointment booking **from baseline to at least 97%** (Measurable/Specific), **by deploying the VRAMS-Mobile app to iPhone, iPad, and Galaxy Android users** (Achievable/Relevant), **within the first year of VRAMS-Mobile operations** (Time-bound)."

**Checklist for each objective you draft:**
- [ ] Names a single, clear outcome (Specific)
- [ ] Includes a number, percentage, or countable target (Measurable)
- [ ] Is realistic given the stated budget/resources/team (Achievable)
- [ ] Explicitly connects to the business case/goal (Relevant)
- [ ] States a deadline or duration (Time-bound)
